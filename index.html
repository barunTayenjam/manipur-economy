The Price of Conflict:
Who Loses, Who Gains in Manipur’s War Economy

Manipur’s ethnic conflict began on 3 May 2023 between Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities. Since early 2026 it has also set Kuki-Zo and Naga groups against each other. This report counts what the conflict has cost so far: 346 reported deaths, 28,899 people still displaced in September 2026, and more than ₹1,200 crore in lost GST revenue. It also looks at who earns from the disruption, mainly drug and arms networks. Every figure is dated and linked to its source.

In short: As of 24 November 2026, Manipur’s conflict has killed 346 people by official and press counts (306 official to 31 August 2026, plus 40 reported since), left 28,899 people displaced, shut the state for 523 of 1,225 days, and cost over ₹1,200 crore in GST revenue. The UNC highway blockade, suspended on 26 September 2026, remains suspended. Details and sources are in the chapters below.

1Three Phases of the Conflict

The conflict has changed shape three times since May 2023. Each phase brought a different set of people into it, and changed who was losing income and who was earning it.

The fighting started between Meitei and Kuki-Zo groups. Since February 2026 it has also set Kuki-Zo and Naga groups against each other.

I
The Open War
May 2023 – Feb 2025

Meitei ↔ Kuki-Zo violence

Ethnic violence split the state along the valley–hill line. Curfews, highway blockades and mass displacement became daily life, and drug and arms networks expanded while the police were overstretched.

258killed (govt, Nov 2024; now 306) 60,000peak displaced
II
The Interregnum
Feb 2025 – Feb 2026

President’s Rule lull

CM N. Biren Singh resigns and the Centre takes direct control. Stolen militia arms are partly returned and the Kuki SoO is renewed — yet not one displaced family is resettled.

13months of central rule
III
The Fracture Spreads
Mar 2026 – present

Kuki ↔ Naga escalation

A new BJP government under Y. Khemchand Singh takes office and opens the first Kuki-Zo dialogue in three years — even as a fresh Kuki–Naga axis ignites across Kangpokpi, Senapati and Ukhrul.

60+killed in 2026 (Biren Singh, Sep) 85FIRs · 5 zones

3Who Loses: Farmers, Workers, Traders and Tourism

This chapter goes sector by sector through the people who have lost income since May 2023: farmers, daily-wage workers, transporters, tourism businesses and young people looking for work.

The losses fall hardest on people with the least to fall back on: farmers who cannot reach their fields, daily-wage workers with no work, and traders cut off from supplies.

4Who Gains: Drug, Arms and Security Networks

Some networks earn more because of the conflict, not in spite of it. This chapter covers drug traffickers, arms suppliers, security contractors and media that grows on conflict coverage.

Drug traffickers, arms suppliers and protection networks are paid whether or not peace returns. That income does not depend on a settlement.

7The Bandh & Blockade Ledger — 523 Days Disrupted

From 3 May 2023 to 8 September 2026, 523 of 1,225 days saw a bandh, blockade or shutdown, according to the State Home Department. That is 43% of the period, before curfews and internet suspensions are counted.

Shutdowns were not occasional. In 2026 they became close to continuous from mid-May until the UNC suspension on 26 September.

11Outlook: What Happens Next

Four conclusions from the figures in this report, and the signs that would show they are changing.

Three years on, there is no settlement in sight, and 2026 has brought more disruption than any year before it.

01

2026 has been the most disrupted year so far

The State Home Department counted 225 disruption days in 2026 to 8 September, more than in 2023, 2024 or 2025. Kuki-Zo and Naga groups also began fighting each other, and the government published its first official death toll in 19 months.

02

The war economy will outlast the fighting

Fighting can stop faster than the income it created. Drug routes, highway payments and security contracts now have people who depend on them, and they will not disappear with a political settlement.

03

Reconstruction costs rise the longer this lasts

The ₹1,200 crore GST loss is not a one-off; revenue is lost every year the disruption continues. Each year also adds unrepaired houses, closed businesses and workers who have left the state.

04

Large gaps remain in the official data

The government published no death toll for 19 months, and there is still no audited estimate of what the conflict has cost. Several figures in this report are estimates for that reason.

Signals to watch: (1) whether the UNC’s Sep 26 suspension holds through the Sangai Festival (Nov 21–30); (2) whether the ~140% commodity surge unwinds with the highways open; (3) the next official death-toll update against Biren Singh’s 60+ killed in 2026; (4) whether the ₹1,200 cr GST loss figure is revised upward; (5) whether Kuki-Zo government dialogue resumes after the Kalenjang and Kangpokpi killings.

12Quick Answers — Manipur's Economy in 2026

Concise, source-verified answers to the most-asked questions about Manipur's conflict economy, displacement and the 2026 blockade cycle.

Short answers to the questions readers ask most often, each with its source and date. The chapters above have the longer treatment.

What is happening in Manipur right now?

As of 24 November 2026: the UNC economic blockade is suspended but not lifted, Kuki–Naga violence is the front that is escalating, and the reported death toll stands at 346. A 72-hour ultimatum issued by CoTU on 24 November threatens an indefinite shutdown of all highways through Kuki-Zo areas from midnight 27 November.

The blockade was suspended at 6 pm on 26 September after Centre–state–UNC tripartite talks in Guwahati (Tangkhul Express). Since October, Kuki–Naga violence has surged: nine killed Oct 9–11 across Kamjong, Ukhrul and Kangpokpi; a Kuki farmer killed Oct 3; a Kuki-Zo woman killed in a Kamjong ambush Oct 15–16; three Meitei firewood collectors killed Oct 19 in Imphal East; a Kuki-Zo man killed Oct 20 in Kangpokpi; a Chassad villager killed Oct 17 in Kamjong. CorCom called a 12-hr shutdown Oct 15; a 48-hr valley strike Oct 19–20 paralysed Imphal. The Centre deployed 30 more CAPF companies (35,000+ central personnel). Late October: a fourth Sunurok victim died Oct 31, two abducted Kuki-Zo youths were found dead Nov 1, and over 1 lakh rallied in Imphal Oct 30–31 demanding an end to the “proxy war” and abrogation of the SoO pact. November has killed 13 more by press count: three militants in the Nov 4 Toudaijang clash (Noney), a Kuki-Zo woman farmer shot dead Nov 12 while harvesting paddy under security escort at Taphou Kuki (Kangpokpi), and four civilians ambushed Nov 13 at New Heaven on NH-202 (Ukhrul); a suspected KCP (PWG) cadre was killed in a CoBRA encounter at Yurembam, Imphal West (Nov 20), which also netted the outfit’s chairman and seven cadres. CoTU has since declared non-cooperation with security forces in Kangpokpi. Reported deaths push the running total to 346. The CM puts shutdown losses at ₹20–25 crore a day and took the revenue-loss case to Finance Minister Sitharaman on Oct 30. A 24-hour general strike on Nov 17, called over the Nov 13 killings of four Tangkhul Naga civilians, paralysed five valley districts and Naga areas of Ukhrul; the Kuki–Naga phase has claimed 70+ lives since February (TOI Bharat); and separately (not conflict deaths), a spurious-liquor tragedy in Chandel district killed 16–18 people and hospitalised hundreds from Nov 20.

How many people have died in the Manipur conflict?

346 reported (306 official as of Aug 31, 2026 + 40 press-reported Sep 13–Nov 20) and 49 still missing. The official toll of 306 was given by Home Minister Konthoujam to the Assembly on Sep 3 — the first update since Nov 2024's 258. The +40 covers six Kuki-Zo (Sep 13–17), Kalenjang's three Mao Nagas (Sep 26–27), a Kuki farmer (Oct 3), nine in Kamjong/Ukhrul/Kangpokpi (Oct 9–11), a Kuki-Zo woman in Kamjong (Oct 15–16), a Chassad villager (Oct 17), three Meitei firewood collectors (Oct 19), a Kuki-Zo man (Oct 20), a fourth Sunurok victim (Oct 31, Ibomcha), two abducted Kuki-Zo youths found dead Nov 1 (Senapati–Phaibung road), three Kuki ultras killed in the Nov 1 Tazeikaiphun clash (Noney), three militants in the Nov 4 Toudaijang factional clash (Noney), a Kuki-Zo woman farmer killed Nov 12 at Taphou Kuki under security escort (Kangpokpi), four civilians ambushed Nov 13 at New Heaven on NH-202 (Ukhrul) and one suspected KCP (PWG) cadre in the Nov 20 CoBRA encounter (Imphal West). Former CM Biren Singh put 2026 deaths alone at 60+ (Sep 26). The Hindu

Why is the UNC blockading Manipur's highways?

The United Naga Council escalated its blockade over the killing of six Naga civilians at Leilon Vaiphei (Senapati) in May 2026. Demands included the removal of Deputy CM Nemcha Kipgen. The blockade ran roughly four months (May 17 – Sep 26) and was suspended from 6 pm Sep 26 after tripartite talks in Guwahati. Northeast Now · Tangkhul Express

How much have commodity prices risen?

Roughly 140% since the blockade intensified (ET, Aug 2026). Black-market peaks: petrol at ₹250/L (vs ~₹107), LPG at ₹5,000 (vs ~₹900), rice at ₹80/kg in Kangpokpi. Trucks remain stuck on blocked NHs for days. ET, Aug 2026

How many people are displaced?

Displacement peaked at ~60,000 in 2023. As of Sep 17, 2026: 31,823 returned, 28,899 still displaced. 7,034 houses built, 4,159 repaired. At least 731 IDPs have died in camps. (CM's Sep 3 statement used different counting bases: 36,000 resettled / 24,000 in camps.) Northeast Now, Sep 17

What is Manipur's GSDP and fiscal cost of the conflict?

GSDP 2024-25: ₹49,937 crore (+9.6% nominal, masks sectoral collapse). 2026-27 budget projects ₹68,823 crore. The CM revealed a ₹1,200 crore+ GST revenue loss (Sep 16) — the first official fiscal-cost figure. This report's structural estimate of ₹5,000–8,000 cr forgone output remains independent. PRS India

How is the drug economy connected to the conflict?

Heroin and meth from Myanmar's Golden Triangle move through Moreh into Manipur, where policing has been stretched thin since 2023. UNODC estimates ~₹2,500 cr/yr flows through the state; police seized ₹1,600 cr in one year (ORF). Drug income pays for weapons, and the weapons keep the conflict going. ORF

Were the blockades community-specific or economy-wide?

Economy-wide. The blockades closed national highways, the landlocked state’s only freight routes, so goods movement stopped for all communities. More than 2,000 trucks were stranded on NH-2 within days of the May 2026 escalation, and freight costs rose 3–5×. Checkpoints in the buffer zones did stop people by community, but the highway closures themselves affected everyone. ET, Aug 2026

How many days of blockade since May 2023?

523 days of bandhs, shutdowns and economic blockades between May 3, 2023 and Sep 8, 2026 — 43% of all days — per State Home Dept data. Per-year: 107 (2023), 113 (2024), 78 (2025), 225 (2026 alone) — highest on record. Plus 385 days internet suspension, 261 days curfew. Intermittent until mid-May 2026, then effectively continuous. Assam Tribune, Sep 18

Is transport to Manipur recovering?

Partially. The Imphal–Dimapur–Guwahati bus service resumed Aug 21, 2026, ending a three-year suspension — by Oct 5 it was carrying 450–500 passengers a day, the CM said. The UNC blockade was suspended Sep 26, reopening NH-2, NH-202, NH-37 and NH-102A — though CoTU’s Oct 5 Kangpokpi shutdown showed single incidents can still close the highways. Passenger movement and goods movement are on separate tracks.

What did the Supreme Court say?

On Aug 14, 2026, the CJI Surya Kant bench urged Kuki and Meitei groups to make peace and remove highway blockades, directed both sides to furnish comprehensive proposals for clearing all blocked NHs, and warned against a piecemeal approach. The case arose from a plea by a Kuki women's body. On Oct 5, 2026, the Court directed the CBI and trial court to ensure two victim-witnesses in a Manipur sexual-violence case depose on fixed dates, after the CBI flagged witness intimidation. The Hindu · New Indian Express, Oct 5
