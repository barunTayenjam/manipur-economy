The Price of Conflict:
Who Loses, Who Gains in Manipur’s War Economy

Three years of ethnic violence has fractured Manipur’s economy across three communities — Meitei, Kuki-Zo and Naga — destroying livelihoods for the majority while fueling a parallel war economy that profits select networks. As of September 2026, the UNC economic blockade of NH-2 and NH-37 intensifies, commodity prices surge 140%, and essential services remain strained despite partial transport resumption.

1Three Phases of a Fractured State

The conflict did not stay still. Over three years it moved through distinct phases — each reshaping who bore the cost and who captured the gains.

What began as a two-community war is now a three-front crisis — and 2026 is its most violent year yet.

I
The Open War
May 2023 – Feb 2025

Meitei ↔ Kuki-Zo violence

Ethnic violence splits the state along the valley–hill line. Curfews, highway blockades and mass displacement define daily life; a parallel war economy takes root in the vacuum.

258killed (govt, Nov 2024 — now 306) 60,000peak displaced
II
The Interregnum
Feb 2025 – Feb 2026

President’s Rule lull

CM N. Biren Singh resigns and the Centre takes direct control. Stolen militia arms are partly returned and the Kuki SoO is renewed — yet not one displaced family is resettled.

13months of central rule
III
The Fracture Spreads
Mar 2026 – present

Kuki ↔ Naga escalation

A new BJP government under Y. Khemchand Singh takes office and opens the first Kuki-Zo dialogue in three years — even as a fresh Kuki–Naga axis ignites across Kangpokpi, Senapati and Ukhrul.

24killed (Jan–Jul 2026) 85FIRs · 5 zones

11What This Adds Up To — Outlook

The evidence in this report points in one direction. Four conclusions, and the signals that would change them.

The conflict is no longer an emergency to be waited out — it is a structural condition with its own economy, and the data says it is still worsening.

01

2026 is the inflection point

225 disruption days in eight months, a third ethnic axis open, and the first official death toll in 19 months. Every trend line on this site steepens after March 2026.

02

The war economy is stickier than the war

Ceasefires end fighting faster than they end rents. Narcotics flows, blockade tolls and security contracts have fixed constituencies that survive any political settlement.

03

Reconstruction cost is compounding

₹1,200 cr GST forgone is recurring, not one-off. Each year of blockade adds a fresh layer of lost output on top of unrepaired houses, closed firms and out-migrated workers.

04

The missing-data problem is the story

No death toll for 19 months. No audited conflict-cost estimate. Accountability gaps are not an oversight — they are how the status quo holds.

Signals to watch: (1) whether the UNC blockade is lifted or normalized; (2) the next official death-toll update; (3) whether the ₹1,200 cr GST loss figure is revised upward; (4) any resumption of Kuki-Zo government dialogue; (5) harvest-season price spikes under continued NH restrictions.

12Quick Answers — Manipur's Economy in 2026

Concise, source-verified answers to the most-asked questions about Manipur's conflict economy, displacement and the September 2026 blockade.

Each answer is anchored to an official figure or a named source — no speculation.

What is happening in Manipur right now?

As of September 23, 2026, the UNC's indefinite economic blockade continues across NH-2, NH-202, NH-37 and NH-102A — now in its fifth month. Essential-commodity prices have surged ~140%. Six Kuki-Zo civilians were killed Sep 13–17, triggering a fresh NH-37 shutdown. The Imphal–Dimapur–Guwahati bus service resumed Aug 21 after three years. The Hindu

How many people have died in the Manipur conflict?

306 killed and 49 still missing as of Aug 31, 2026 — the first official toll since Nov 2024's figure of 258, given by Home Minister Konthoujam to the Assembly on Sep 3. Excludes six Kuki-Zo killed Sep 13–17. The Hindu

Why is the UNC blockading Manipur's highways?

The United Naga Council escalated its blockade over the killing of six Naga civilians at Leilon Vaiphei (Senapati) in May 2026. Demands include the removal of Deputy CM Nemcha Kipgen. The blockade has run 125+ days with a brief NH-37 lift in mid-September. Northeast Now

How much have commodity prices risen?

Roughly 140% since the blockade intensified (ET, Aug 2026). Black-market peaks: petrol at ₹250/L (vs ~₹107), LPG at ₹5,000 (vs ~₹900), rice at ₹80/kg in Kangpokpi. Trucks remain stuck on blocked NHs for days. ET, Aug 2026

How many people are displaced?

Displacement peaked at ~60,000 in 2023. As of Sep 17, 2026: 31,823 returned, 28,899 still displaced. 7,034 houses built, 4,159 repaired. At least 731 IDPs have died in camps. (CM's Sep 3 statement used different counting bases — 36,000 resettled / 24,000 in camps.) Northeast Now, Sep 17

What is Manipur's GSDP and fiscal cost of the conflict?

GSDP 2024-25: ₹49,937 crore (+9.6% nominal — masks sectoral collapse). 2026-27 budget projects ₹68,823 crore. The CM revealed a ₹1,200 crore+ GST revenue loss (Sep 16) — the first official fiscal-cost figure. This report's structural estimate of ₹5,000–8,000 cr forgone output remains independent. PRS India

How is the drug economy connected to the conflict?

Myanmar's Golden Triangle routes heroin and meth through Moreh into Manipur's governance vacuum. UNODC estimates ~₹2,500 cr/yr flows through the state; police seized ₹1,600 cr in one year (ORF). The conflict's self-funding loop — drugs fund armed groups, armed groups sustain conflict — is the central mechanism. ORF

Were the blockades community-specific or economy-wide?

Economy-wide. The blockades targeted national highways — the land-locked state's only freight arteries — so the economic effect fell on goods movement for all communities. 2,000+ trucks stranded on NH-2 within days of the May 2026 escalation; freight costs 3–5× throughout. Community-specific controls existed at buffer-zone checkpoints, but the blockades themselves were community-agnostic in effect. ET, Aug 2026

How many days of blockade since May 2023?

523 days of bandhs, shutdowns and economic blockades between May 3, 2023 and Sep 8, 2026 — 43% of all days — per State Home Dept data. Per-year: 107 (2023), 113 (2024), 78 (2025), 225 (2026 alone) — highest on record. Plus 385 days internet suspension, 261 days curfew. Intermittent until mid-May 2026, then effectively continuous. Assam Tribune, Sep 18

Is transport to Manipur recovering?

Partially. The Imphal–Dimapur–Guwahati bus service resumed Aug 21, 2026, ending a three-year suspension. But freight on NH-2, NH-37, NH-202 and NH-102A remains disrupted by the UNC blockade and periodic shutdowns (e.g., JTCM's NH-37 closure, Sep 10–11). Passenger movement and goods movement are on separate tracks.

What did the Supreme Court say?

On Aug 14, 2026, the CJI Surya Kant bench urged Kuki and Meitei groups to make peace and remove highway blockades, directed both sides to furnish comprehensive proposals for clearing all blocked NHs, and warned against a piecemeal approach. The case arose from a plea by a Kuki women's body. The Hindu · LiveLaw
